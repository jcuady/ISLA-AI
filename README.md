## Short description

An employee pastes a customer record into an AI tool. Everything the bank has written about that
being forbidden is undone in the time it takes to paste.

KALIX is a fully on-device copilot that makes that impossible to get wrong. It has two working
controls:

1. **Egress Guard** — detects and redacts 18 classes of Philippine banking PII *before* it can
   leave, then **re-runs the entire detector over its own output** and escalates rather than
   releasing if anything survives. Fails closed.
2. **DPA Copilot** — answers Data Privacy Act questions in Taglish from seven real Philippine
   legal instruments, **quoted from cited spans**, and refuses when the evidence is thin instead of
   inventing a circular number.

No cloud inference. No customer data leaves the machine. Every claim is generated by
running committed code.

## Why does this product benefit from running AI locally?

This is the question the brief asks every submission to answer, and for KALIX it is not a
preference — it is the difference between a working control and a fictional one.

**1. Using a cloud model to find PII requires first sending the PII.**
To classify a record you must transmit it. The act of asking an external model "is this sensitive
personal information?" is itself a disclosure of that information to a third party, in a foreign
jurisdiction, with no contractual relationship to the data subject. Under RA 10173 the controller
remains accountable for that disclosure. So the naive "scan it with an API" design *is* the breach
it is meant to prevent — it just does the leaking in the first step.

**2. The target environment has no network, by design.**
Philippine banks run air-gapped core-banking estates and ISO 27001-sealed networks where the
post-incident response window can be measured in hours. In that window a network call is
unavailable *by policy* — the control must work with the cable out, not degrade when it is pulled.
KALIX is demonstrated working with the network disconnected.

**3. The economics do not work per-paste.**
Scanning every paste across a branch network is a high-frequency, low-value-per-call workload. At
API pricing that is a permanent line item; locally it is arithmetic we already own. KALIX's PII
scan measures **0.2 ms p50** with no per-call cost.

**4. Regulators are already asking this question of banks.**
NPC Advisory 2024-04 applies the Data Privacy Act to AI systems that process personal data. Every
Philippine bank is already an AI company under it — credit scoring, KYC, collections. Almost none
can prove what their AI did, to which records, on which date, without shipping the evidence off
the machine. KALIX produces that proof **locally**, as a hash-chained ledger that stores entity
counts and verdicts and never the customer text.

**5. Confidentiality is not a promise you can audit.**
"Read our DPA" is a contractual claim. "No module on the request path opens a network connection
except the air-gap probe itself, the browser is forbidden from fetching off-origin by a
`default-src 'none'` CSP that CI asserts on every push, and the PII engine imports nothing outside
the standard library" is a verifiable one. This repository ships all three as tests.

Being precise matters here, so we will not claim more than is true: KALIX *does* open a socket —
`/api/airgap` connects to fixed public resolvers on every probe, sending a TCP handshake and no
payload, precisely so that pulling the ethernet cable flips the indicator. That is the only
outbound traffic the product generates.

The honest counterweight: local does **not** mean better. A 3B model is a 3B model. KALIX's
correctness comes from **retrieval, not scale** — answers are quoted from cited spans, so the
worst case is a wrong chunk rather than an invented statute. That trade is documented in
[`docs/DISCLOSURES.md`](docs/DISCLOSURES.md).

## What it does

### Egress Guard

Built for the Philippines, not translated into it. Generic PII models miss the things Philippine
bank branches actually type:

| Detected | Why a generic model misses it |
|---|---|
| SSS in `3-2-4`, `2-3-4`, `3-3-3` layouts | Non-canonical formats are invisible to a pattern trained on one shape |
| GCash number | Literally a mobile number with no marker — indistinguishable from an ordinary contact |
| TIN vs SSS disambiguation | Both are 9 digits; only the leading-zero and checksum patterns separate them |
| Remittance references | Bank-specific `REMIT-` prefixes |
| Card PAN + CVV + expiry as a **credential bundle** | Three entities that are individually ambiguous and jointly catastrophic |

Then the part that matters: **verification**. After redaction, KALIX re-runs the full detector
over its own output. Any surviving PII escalates instead of shipping. Tokens are masked between
passes so the verifier cannot rediscover its own output.

### DPA Copilot

Hybrid retrieval — dense (`multilingual-e5-small` int8 ONNX) + BM25 + section-ID matching,
weighted `0.60 / 0.25 / 0.15` and renormalised when the dense leg is absent — over **223 citable
chunks from 7 real instruments**. Answers are constrained to quoted spans, every claim carries its
section, and a strict lexical domain gate refuses out-of-domain questions.

It speaks Taglish because that is the working language of a Philippine branch:

> *"Ilang oras dapat ko i-report ang data breach?"*
> → **Within 72 hours from knowledge of the personal data breach**, based on available information.
> `[NPC-CIRC-16-03]`

> *"Pwede ba ipasa ang CDR ng customer ko sa vendor namin sa Singapore?"*
> → A personal information controller is accountable for information transferred to a third party,
> whether domestically or internationally. `[RA-10173 §21]`

> *"Who won the 2025 FIFA World Cup?"*
> → *Hindi ito tanong tungkol sa Data Privacy Act o mga patakaran ng NPC, kaya wala akong
> maibibigay na batayan sa loob ng corpus na 'to.* — **and it cites nothing.**

### Audit Ledger

Every action writes a SHA-256 hash-chained record. Each entry is linked to the last, so altering
one breaks every entry after it and anyone can verify the chain in a single pass. **The ledger
stores counts, verdicts and latency — never customer text.**

## Quick start

Python **3.12** is required — 3.14 has no wheels for `transformers`, `gliner`, `optimum` or
`llama-cpp-python`.

```powershell
# 1. Environment
uv python install 3.12
uv venv --python 3.12 .venv
uv pip install --python .venv\Scripts\python.exe torch --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cpu
uv pip install --python .venv\Scripts\python.exe -r requirements.txt

# 2. Model weights (embeddings first, then optional LLM, then optional NER)
.venv\Scripts\python.exe models\download_models.py

# 3. Corpus (public NPC/BSP documents via mirrors)
.venv\Scripts\python.exe corpus\fetch_corpus.py
.venv\Scripts\python.exe corpus\chunk_corpus.py

# 4. UI
npm --prefix apps\web install
npm --prefix apps\web run build

# 5. Run — binds 127.0.0.1 only
.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m uvicorn services.core.app:app --host 127.0.0.1 --port 8765
```

Open <http://127.0.0.1:8765> for the landing page, or go straight to the console at
<http://127.0.0.1:8765/app>. Or run [`scripts/bootstrap.ps1`](scripts/bootstrap.ps1) to do all of it.

### Verify it yourself

```powershell
# 101 Python tests + 95 UI tests
.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pytest tests/ -q
npm --prefix apps\web test
npm --prefix apps\web run typecheck

# Regenerate the scoreboard from the committed datasets
.venv\Scripts\python.exe eval\run_eval.py

# What the local neural leg actually contributes (needs the ONNX weights).
# This is the measurement behind the "is local AI fundamental?" answer, and it
# is what caught the mean-pooling bug.
.venv\Scripts\python.exe eval\retrieval_ablation.py

# The no-weights path — proves the copilot works with no model weights at all
pip install -r requirements-verify.txt
python eval\run_eval.py --sparse-only

# 39 assertions covering every claim the demo makes
.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\preflight.py

# Both web surfaces: console errors, external requests, overflow, CSP hash
npm --prefix apps\web install
node apps\web\verify-ui.mjs
```

CI runs all of it on every push: [`.github/workflows/verify.yml`](.github/workflows/verify.yml).

> **The PII engine's CI job installs no dependencies at all.** `services/pii/` imports nothing
> outside the Python standard library, so that job fails the moment someone hides an ML dependency
> in the Egress Guard hot path.

## What runs locally · what requires internet

| | |
|---|---|
| **Runs locally, always** | Egress Guard · DPA Copilot · audit ledger · hash pseudonyms · air-gap probe · both web surfaces |
| **Requires internet — first build only** | Model weight download (Qwen2.5-3B GGUF, multilingual-e5 ONNX, optional GLiNER) · corpus fetch from public NPC/lawphil mirrors |
| **Requires internet — never, at runtime** | Nothing. No customer data leaves the machine and no cloud service is called. The only outbound traffic KALIX generates is the air-gap probe's own empty TCP handshake to fixed public resolvers. Both web surfaces ship `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'`; `verify-ui.mjs` asserts zero external requests on every run. |
| **Cloud AI APIs** | **None.** No OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, or any other model API is used anywhere in the product. |

The `NETWORK:` chip in the console header reports an **actual socket probe** — pull the cable and
the answer changes in front of you. It is not a hardcoded green badge.

## How we built it — and what went wrong

Full transparency, because the failures are the most useful part of this project.

| Problem we hit | What actually happened | What we did |
|---|---|---|
| **The corpus fetcher silently saved garbage** | Five "circulars" turned out to be 238 kB index pages, and `HTTP 200` was all we checked | Added a content-validation gate that rejects index pages masquerading as documents. Never trust a status code. |
| **Every citation was off by one** | The chunker labelled each chunk with the *following* section's heading | Rewrote header→body association and re-validated all 223 chunks. |
| **A 500 on every copilot query** | `numpy.float32` is not JSON-serialisable; FastAPI's encoder rejected it | Coerced at every boundary: `RetrievedChunk.to_dict()`, `copilot._native_float()`, ledger `default=str`. |
| **The Singapore question scored BM25 0.000** | A Taglish question about sending data to a vendor shares *no content word* with the statute's phrasing, so retrieval fell back to an unrelated provision | Added `expand_query()` mapping outbound phrasing onto statutory vocabulary. Now resolves to RA-10173 §21. |
| **BM25 was scoring Taglish particles** | Five of twelve tokens in a typical question were `ba`/`ng`/`ang`/`mag` | Added a Taglish stoplist and a conservative stemmer (`register`/`registration` collapse to `regist`). |
| **The landing page went blank if JS broke** | Sections were `opacity:0` by default, so a CSP mismatch would silently erase the page | Content is now hidden *only* when JS is confirmed running — progressive enhancement. |
| **Our own CSP blocked our own script** | `default-src 'none'` with no `script-src` | Permitted by exact SHA-256 hash, in both the page meta and the server header; CI now fails if either goes stale. |
| **Python 3.14 was unusable** | Zero wheels for `transformers`, `gliner`, `presidio`, `llama-cpp`, `optimum` | Pinned 3.12 and documented why in `scripts/bootstrap.ps1`. |
| **The audit ledger was flooded by its own health check** | The console re-probes the network every 20 s so the air-gap claim can be falsified live, and every probe wrote a ledger entry — about **4,300 identical entries a day**, burying the `pii_redaction` and `copilot_answer` records the trail exists to prove | The probe still runs on every call; the ledger now records a **change** in outbound reachability, not each poll. An unchanged state is not an auditable event. Covered by `tests/test_audit_events.py`. |
| **Two status tones shared one icon** | `refuse` and `neutral` both rendered `HelpCircle`, so desaturating the UI made two different states identical — quietly breaking the four-channel status guarantee | Caught by a test that asserts every tone has a distinct glyph. `neutral` now uses `CircleDashed`. The test, not the component, was the arbiter. |
| **`?view=egress` was a dead link** | Navigation lived only in React state, so a shared URL always reopened the Copilot and Back did nothing | The active control now lives in the URL, with an unknown value falling back to the Copilot instead of rendering a blank screen. |
| **We were mean-pooling an e5 model** | `intfloat/multilingual-e5-small` requires **CLS pooling** per its model card. With BERT-style mean pooling the dense leg scored *below* a lexical-only baseline — it was actively hurting retrieval, and no headline metric noticed | Added `eval/retrieval_ablation.py`, which ranks the correct instrument per question with and without the model. It failed first: paraphrase MRR 0.552 vs 0.572. Fixed to CLS pooling, re-encoded, re-measured: 0.578 vs 0.572. Published metrics were unchanged throughout — which is exactly why the ablation was needed. |
| **We claimed we open no socket at all** | `/api/airgap` opens a real TCP connection to fixed public resolvers on every probe, because a judge must be able to falsify the badge by pulling the cable | Restated the claim accurately in seven documents and enforced it: `tests/test_airgap_claims.py` fails if any runtime module outside the probe gains a network primitive. |

**Judgement calls we made against our own convenience:** we did not tune BM25 until the one failing
evaluation case went green. It is a corpus coverage gap, and we would rather publish a red number
we can explain than a green one nobody can trust.

## Known limitations

Stated plainly, because a compliance tool that overstates itself is worse than no tool.

- **Source attribution is 80%, not 100%.** One question in five attributes its answer to the wrong
  instrument. Cause: corpus coverage, not reasoning. See `docs/DISCLOSURES.md` §7.
- **Two legal instruments could not be retrieved.** NPC Circular 2016-03 (outbound transfer) and
  BSP Memorandum M-2024-019 are not publicly reachable; the outbound-transfer question is answered
  from RA-10173 §26 instead. Documented in `docs/CORPUS_SOURCES.md`.
- **The corpus is seven instruments.** Breadth is the single highest-value improvement available.
- **The local LLM did not finish downloading** (2,007 of 2,105 MB; Hugging Face throttled this
  host). The product is fully functional without it — every number above was measured without it,
  and extractive answering is the more defensible posture for a compliance tool anyway.
- **Retrieval embeddings are the small model.** `multilingual-e5-small` was chosen for a 15 GB
  RAM laptop with no GPU. Its similarity scores for legal text are compressed into a narrow band,
  which is why the refusal gate is lexical rather than similarity-based.
